Glossary · What the words mean

Pass
The facility can operate normally. This does not mean zero violations: a facility can pass with several minor ones (some pass with 8 or more). The rating depends on how serious the violations are and whether they were corrected, not on a count. There is no fixed number of "allowed" violations.
Conditional Pass
The inspector found serious violations. The facility may keep operating, but under orders to fix the problems, and an inspector comes back to check. Think of it as "on notice".
Closure
The inspector found an immediate health hazard (for example a vermin infestation or no hot water). The facility's permit is suspended and it must shut its doors until the problem is fixed and verified.
Violations count
How many individual problems the inspector recorded on that visit, from minor (a wall that needs repainting) to major (food at unsafe temperatures). Severity matters more than the number.
Failure
Our word for an inspection that ended in Conditional Pass or Closure: the visits where enforcement kicked in.
Re-rated
After a failure, the next inspection that assigned a new grade. This is how we know whether the facility responded.
Passed re-rating
That follow-up inspection ended in a Pass: the facility fixed the problem, at least for now.
Held at next check
At the next graded inspection after the fix, often months later, the facility was still passing. Our measure of whether the fix lasted.
n=
The number of cases behind a statistic. A rate computed from 12 facilities means much less than one computed from 1,200, so every rate on this site shows its n. Groups too small to trust show their count instead of a rate.
Inspection types
Routine: a regular unannounced visit. Reinspection: a follow-up after a problem. Complaint: triggered by a public complaint. Foodborne Illness: investigating a report of someone getting sick. New Ownership: checking a facility that changed hands. Type pending: the city publishes this label up to a year late, so recent visits show it as pending.

Read the numbers with these caveats

  • One row per inspection event after latest-record dedup; farmers-market umbrella permits are excluded from facility timelines.
  • Inspection types are backfilled by DPH with roughly a year’s lag, so recent “Reinspection” labels are incomplete. The funnel uses ratings, which do not lag.
  • A failure with no later rated visit is censored, not resolved: registry cross-checks show at least 13 of 150 such facilities closed for good.
  • Post-enforcement facilities fail their next check at roughly 3× the base rate; the data cannot say whether that is enforcement failing or selection of trouble-prone facilities.
