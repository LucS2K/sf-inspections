01 · The record

The city never stops checking

Inspection cadence is steady, month after month. Enforcement begins when one of these visits goes wrong: most failures are Conditional Passes; Closure is the rarer, sharper instrument.

02 · After a failure

The system responds fast

Each failure followed to its next rated visit, then the one after. “Held” is measured only where a later visit exists; silence is censoring, not success.

03 · Where it clusters

Failure isn’t evenly spread

Share of rated inspections ending in Conditional Pass or Closure. Hover the map for numbers; click a neighborhood for its full picture. Hatched areas and neighborhoods with under 100 rated inspections stay in the table; rates on small samples mislead.

04 · The null result

Yelp can’t tell

Present-day ratings barely differ by enforcement history; the near-identical bars are the finding. Correlation only, on a zero-based 5-star scale. Coverage differs: 86% of enforcement-history facilities match on Yelp vs 67% of the never-failed group, which includes markets and cafeterias Yelp doesn’t list.

Before you quote these numbers

The fine print

Caveats
  • One row per inspection event after latest-record dedup; farmers-market umbrella permits are excluded from facility timelines.
  • Inspection types are backfilled by DPH with roughly a year’s lag, so recent “Reinspection” labels are incomplete. The funnel uses ratings, which do not lag.
  • A failure with no later rated visit is censored, not resolved: registry cross-checks show at least 13 of 150 such facilities closed for good.
  • Post-enforcement facilities fail their next check at roughly 3× the base rate; the data cannot say whether that is enforcement failing or selection of trouble-prone facilities.
Glossary
Pass
The facility can operate normally. This does not mean zero violations: a facility can pass with several minor ones. The rating depends on how serious the violations are and whether they were corrected, not on a count.
Conditional Pass
The inspector found serious violations. The facility may keep operating, but under orders to fix the problems, and an inspector comes back to check. Think of it as “on notice”.
Closure
The inspector found an immediate health hazard (for example a vermin infestation or no hot water). The facility’s permit is suspended and it must shut its doors until the problem is fixed and verified.
Violations count
How many individual problems the inspector recorded on that visit, from minor to major. Severity matters more than the number.
Failure
Our word for an inspection that ended in Conditional Pass or Closure: the visits where enforcement kicked in.
Re-rated
After a failure, the next inspection that assigned a new grade. This is how we know whether the facility responded.
Held at next check
At the next rated inspection after the fix, often months later, the facility was still passing. Our measure of whether the fix lasted.
n=
The number of cases behind a statistic. Every rate on this site shows its n; groups too small to trust show their count instead of a rate.
