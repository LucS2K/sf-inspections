A restaurant fails inspection.
Then what?

When a food business fails a health inspection or is shut down, does it get fixed, does the fix last, or does the place just close?

Read the findings report · How this was built

Using the health inspection data San Francisco publishes on DataSF, we crunched 20,000+ inspections down to that one question. Three findings carry the answer.

Bottom line: the health department responds fast and businesses mostly fix the problem: after a shutdown the median re-check comes the next day, and about 8 in 10 failing businesses pass that re-check. Making fixes stick is the weak link: roughly 1 in 5 fixes fails again within a year, and about 120 failures were never re-checked at all.

01 · The record

The city never stops checking

Inspection cadence is steady, month after month. Enforcement begins when one of these visits goes wrong: most failures are Conditional Passes; Closure is the rarer, sharper instrument.

Every chart on this page re-slices with the time period and neighborhood filters in the top bar.

What do those failures actually cite? They are pile-ons, not single issues. Share of the 1,126 failures citing each problem:

71%cleaning and sanitizing
69%vermin
59%temperature control
33%food storage

The typical failure spans 4.3 distinct problem categories, and 81% of closures cite a vermin infestation. Yet relapse barely varies by category: repeat failure tracks the facility, not the violation. Full-record figures, unaffected by the filters above. More in the findings report.

02 · After a failure

The system responds fast

Each failure is followed to its next inspection, then the one after. A fix only counts as proven where a later inspection exists; no news means unknown, not fixed.

Before you quote these numbers

The fine print

Caveats
  • Each inspection counts once: when the city republishes a corrected record, only the latest version is analyzed. Farmers-market master permits (one permit covering many stalls) are excluded from per-facility histories.
  • Inspection types are backfilled by DPH with roughly a year’s lag, so recent “Reinspection” labels are incomplete. The funnel uses ratings, which do not lag.
  • A failure with no later inspection is an unknown outcome, not a resolved one: registry cross-checks show at least 13 of 150 such facilities closed for good.
  • Facilities that have been through enforcement fail their next check at roughly 3× the base rate; the data cannot say whether that is enforcement failing or enforcement simply concentrating on trouble-prone facilities.
Glossary
Pass
The facility can operate normally. This does not mean zero violations: a facility can pass with several minor ones. The rating depends on how serious the violations are and whether they were corrected, not on a count.
Conditional Pass
The inspector found serious violations. The facility may keep operating, but under orders to fix the problems, and an inspector comes back to check. Think of it as “on notice”.
Closure
The inspector found an immediate health hazard (for example a vermin infestation or no hot water). The facility’s permit is suspended and it must shut its doors until the problem is fixed and verified.
Violations count
How many individual problems the inspector recorded on that visit, from minor to major. Severity matters more than the number.
Failure
Our word for an inspection that ended in Conditional Pass or Closure: the visits where enforcement kicked in.
Re-rated
After a failure, the next inspection that assigned a new grade. This is how we know whether the facility responded.
Held at next check
At the next rated inspection after the fix, often months later, the facility was still passing. Our measure of whether the fix lasted.
n=
The number of cases behind a statistic. Every rate on this site shows its n; groups too small to trust show their count instead of a rate.

The Yelp footnote: review scores barely move with enforcement history. Never-flagged facilities average 3.99 stars (n=568); ever-closed facilities average 3.85 (n=306), a 0.14-star gap on a 5-star scale. Review scores do not reveal kitchen hygiene; this record is the only signal there is. Correlation only, with coverage caveats in the report.
